# Syllabus: from zero to A1

**Status: active, v3, 2026-09-29.** Hayk asked Claude to self-review v2 and finalise it; see DECISIONS.md #13 for what changed and why. Research behind it: `_knowledge/2026-09-29_syllabus-research.md`.

## Basis (decided with Hayk 2026-09-29)

- **Starting point: complete beginner. Goal for now: A1.** B1 is the long-term direction (see "Later" below), not the current plan.
- **Purpose:** daily life, no exam. The priorities are **work, classes and friends**. Admin (flat, doctor, offices) comes later, or just in time when a real situation comes up.
- **Start at unit 0**, with no placement test.
- **Companion:** DW *Nicos Weg* A1 (free videos with native speakers; lessons in `reference/dw_nicos_weg_lessons.txt`). The chat and the app are the main course.
- **Explanations:** in English, with Russian/Armenian comparisons where they help.
- **Corrections:** hint first (see CLAUDE.md).
- **Daily time varies,** so units are budgeted in hours, not days.
- Grammar order follows the consensus of Schritte, Menschen and Nicos Weg. Adjective endings stay at A2 even though Nicos Weg teaches them in A1.

## How a unit runs

1. **Intro in the chat:** the unit's situations, core phrases and grammar, with Russian/Armenian comparisons. Short, and always with Hayk producing German straight away.
2. **Words:** about 80-100 per unit (about 50 in unit 0) from the Goethe A1 list, added to the app's word bank. The app introduces 10 new words a day by default.
3. **Daily core, even on busy days:** word reviews in the app, 5-10 min.
4. **On longer days:**
   - practice in the chat (drills, then role-play);
   - one app test;
   - the unit's *Nicos Weg* lessons (watch, then repeat the lines aloud).
5. **Mid-unit and end-of-unit app tests.** From unit 2 on, every test mixes in about 20% of items from earlier units.
6. **End task:** a real-life task that Claude grades.
7. **The unit is done when** the end task is done and reviewed, the end-of-unit test is at 80% or more after Claude's grading, and all the unit's words have been introduced. Words don't need to be mastered before moving on; spaced repetition carries them.

**Pronunciation:** Claude can't hear Hayk. It comes from the browser voice (speaker buttons in the app), *Nicos Weg*, and repeating aloud. Checking recordings is a later option (DEFERRED_TODO.md).

## Where Russian and Armenian help, and where they don't

- **Russian:** the case idea (accusative, dative), "Вы" = "Sie", где/куда = wo/wohin, "у меня болит" = "mir tut ... weh", which is the same "to me it hurts" structure.
- **Not from Russian:** articles, gender that often differs (das Mädchen, die Sonne vs солнце), and verb second. Prepositions after verbs don't carry over (warten **auf**).
- **Armenian:** the verb at the end in the verb bracket (ich **kann** heute nicht **kommen**) feels natural from SOV word order. Armenian has no gender, so der/die/das is the main memory load. Learn every noun with its article from day one.
- **Pronunciation:** German spelling is much more regular than English. Unit 0 is short but important: after it, you can read any German word aloud correctly.
